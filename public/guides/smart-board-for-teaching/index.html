Smart Board for Teaching: Setup, Lessons and Free Software

A smart board for teaching earns its place when students can follow an explanation and the teacher can respond without fighting the controls. Test the writing, viewing and saving workflow before choosing hardware or software.

By Kopy Notes contributors · Updated 11 October 2026

Which smartboard features matter in a classroom?

Use an actual teaching task as your acceptance test. Open a page, write a sentence, draw a diagram, erase part of it and move to the next page. Repeat near the edges of the display and check visibility from the back row.

  • Responsiveness: handwriting should follow the pen without a distracting delay. Test imported material as well as an empty page.
  • Touch and stylus: try resting your hand while writing, two-finger panning and pinch zoom. Palm rejection depends on what the hardware and operating system report.
  • Readable display: check small labels from students' seats. Use strong contrast and enough empty space instead of tiny notes.
  • Connections: verify display, touch USB, audio and available inputs. A video connection alone may not carry touch.
  • Storage: decide where editable lessons and backups live. Reopening a saved lesson must be part of the test.

You can try digital teaching without buying a new interactive display. A laptop with a projector and a suitable pen tablet can be a useful starting point. Assess your classroom, accessibility requirements and available budget before choosing equipment.

Set up Kopy Notes on web, Windows or Android

  1. Web: open the free board, choose your teaching defaults and create a lesson. No account is required for the local workflow. Use the same browser profile next time.
  2. Windows: choose the Windows installer from GitHub Releases. Read the release notes, check the checksum and try a short lesson before teaching with a development build.
  3. Android: choose the APK from the same release page and check your device's installation policy. Development APKs are test-signed, not Play Store certified. Test touch, import and saving on your smartboard.
  4. Offline: open the app, import material and exercise your tools while connected. Then test with the network disconnected. A first browser visit needs uncached app resources.

Current native source includes device-folder backups and display annotation; check release notes for availability in your download. Keep independent exported copies outside browser storage.

Three classroom scenarios to try

Annotate a PDF and add a worked example

Create a lesson and import a PDF as page-by-page slides. Choose orientation, placement and margins, then check the first and last pages. During class, highlight a diagram, write short annotations and add a blank page for a worked solution. Keep the source PDF separate from the annotated lesson.

Teach a graph through prediction

Ask students to sketch y = sin(x), then use the Graph tool to compare their predictions with the curve. Keep the same x range when comparing y = cos(x). Ask what changed, where the intercepts moved and which features stayed the same. The plotted answer should begin a discussion, not finish it.

Use geometry for a short challenge

Draw a triangle and use a ruler or protractor while explaining the construction. Give pairs a timed question and reveal the answer after they commit to a prediction. The tool makes a step visible; explain why the construction works as well as how to perform it.

Save an editable lesson for another class

PDF shares the finished pages. An editable .kopy file preserves supported text, shapes, ink and original attachments so you can adjust the lesson next week. Keep both when students need a handout and you need a reusable document.

Download Graph investigation (.kopy) and import it into Kopy Notes. Replace its prompts with your own topic. It includes a prediction page and comparison page with annotation space. This is a teaching template, not an already plotted graph.

Make a smartboard useful with limited internet

  • Prepare and import the material while connected. Test offline before the lesson.
  • Prefer local material to an external website or live video during the explanation.
  • Close unnecessary tabs and apps. On a lower-memory device, split a large presentation into smaller lessons.
  • Export complex presentations to PDF when editable import changes the appearance.
  • Save an editable copy outside browser storage. Keep a second copy on another drive or your school's backed-up folder.
  • Practise reopening the lesson after restarting the device. Recovery belongs in the teaching workflow.

Kopy Notes is an independent, open-source alternative to Note3 and Note5. It remains a development project: test your pen, documents and device before relying on a build for an entire school day.

Smartboard teaching questions

Does the board need Windows?

Kopy Notes has a web workflow and development Windows and Android builds. Choose a version that fits your hardware and test it locally. Browser and Android vendor restrictions vary.

Can I use existing slides?

Basic PPTX and ODP import is available, but advanced layouts, animations and charts are not fully preserved. Export to PDF when the original appearance matters.

Can Kopy replace Note3 or Note5?

Try your everyday import, writing and saving tasks first. The project is independent and does not promise complete feature parity or lossless compatibility.
