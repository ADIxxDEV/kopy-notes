Online Whiteboard for Free: A Practical Guide for Teachers

You can use an online whiteboard for free without making every lesson depend on a subscription. Choose a tool that handles your material, keeps lessons editable and fits your classroom connection.

By Kopy Notes contributors · Updated 11 October 2026

What should a free online whiteboard do for teachers?

Start with the actions you repeat every day: prepare a page, explain an idea, annotate an answer and save the result for the next class. A useful board keeps these actions simple as your lesson library grows.

  • Clear handwriting: pen, highlighter, eraser and undo should be easy to reach. Test your actual stylus and palm behavior instead of relying on a feature label.
  • Editable lessons: preserve text, shapes and annotations in a format you can reopen. A screenshot is useful for sharing but cannot preserve every editable object.
  • Material import: try one of your PDFs and slide decks. Complex fonts, diagrams or animations may need a PDF export from the original presentation app.
  • Classroom widgets: timers, graphs and geometry tools should support the explanation without taking over the writing surface.
  • An offline plan: check what works after the app is loaded, and keep a separate copy of your lesson files.

Kopy Notes combines these workflows in a free, open-source development app. Your local library does not require an account. Live collaboration and automatic account-based cloud sync are not currently promised features.

Try a 15-minute fractions lesson

  1. Before class: create a Fractions warm-up lesson or import the sample below. Choose a high-contrast background and write the learning objective.
  2. Minutes 1-3: ask which is larger, one half or one third. Let students predict before revealing the answer.
  3. Minutes 4-8: draw equal wholes with the Shape tool. Shade them in different colors and annotate students' reasoning.
  4. Minutes 9-12: add a page beside your explanation. Give a short comparison problem and use a timer if it helps.
  5. Minutes 13-15: write an exit ticket, export a PDF for sharing and save an editable .kopy copy for the next class.

The same routine works for vocabulary, a circuit sketch or a map. Prepare reusable warm-up, explanation and practice pages instead of rebuilding an empty board every morning. Keep your examples readable from the back of the room, and use color to show a relationship rather than decorate every sentence.

Set up Kopy Notes on web, Windows or Android

  1. Web: open the free board, choose your teaching defaults and create a lesson. No account is required for the local workflow. Use the same browser profile next time.
  2. Windows: choose the Windows installer from GitHub Releases. Read the release notes, check the checksum and try a short lesson before teaching with a development build.
  3. Android: choose the APK from the same release page and check your device's installation policy. Development APKs are test-signed, not Play Store certified. Test touch, import and saving on your smartboard.
  4. Offline: open the app, import material and exercise your tools while connected. Then test with the network disconnected. A first browser visit needs uncached app resources.

Current native source includes device-folder backups and display annotation; check release notes for availability in your download. Keep independent exported copies outside browser storage.

Download free editable lesson templates

Download Fractions warm-up (.kopy) or Observe, predict, explain (.kopy). Each contains two editable pages with original prompts and annotation space. Create a lesson in Kopy Notes, choose Import and select the downloaded .kopy file. Import creates a separate lesson so the original sample stays unchanged.

Replace the prompts, colors and shapes with your own examples. Save your adapted copy with a clear subject and title, then keep an independent backup outside the browser. The samples contain no student information.

Questions about a free online whiteboard

Is Kopy Notes free without a subscription?

The current app is free to use and the core source is MIT licensed. Third-party artwork has separate terms. Planned premium themes are not a current teaching requirement.

Can an online whiteboard work offline?

Supported local features can work after the app and resources are cached. Test the exact lesson offline first; a new browser visit cannot load uncached resources without a connection.

Will lessons survive clearing browser data?

Browser-only copies can be removed when site data is cleared. Export editable .kopy files and keep backups on your device or another drive.
